In Japan, communicating in English isn't always enough.

Many Japanese companies can work in English. But when your conversation is one English thread among projects running in Japanese, it can slip down the list — unless the other side has a strong reason to prioritize it.

When replies stop, one phone call often works faster than another email. One visit can change a relationship more than several online meetings. And sometimes, the right move is to wait.

With someone on the Japan side, small judgments and actions like these keep things moving.

Surfacing hidden barriers — and reporting back to HQ

Deals don't stall on price alone. An unclear explanation in Japanese, internal coordination on the customer's side, or a lack of information for the decision-maker — I pick up what isn't visible on the surface by talking directly with the Japan side.

Then I sort out whether it's specific to one company or a pattern across the market, and report back to HQ — feeding into your sales materials or PR when useful.
