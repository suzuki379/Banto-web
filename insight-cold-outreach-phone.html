Cold Outreach in Japan: Make It Easy for the Person Who Picks Up

When you call a Japanese company for cold outreach, are you factoring in how easy you're making it for the person who picks up the phone to act on your request?

It sounds like a small thing. But it's often the difference between a message reaching someone and it quietly disappearing.

What Happens to an Email Sent to info@

Here's what tends to happen with a cold email to info@: whoever manages that inbox sees something that looks like a sales pitch, and decides not to forward it. Not rudeness — just a low-stakes call not to bother a colleague with something unsolicited.

A Phone Call Changes the Calculation

Asking directly — "could you forward this to the right person, or share their email?" — gives the person a clear, easy action to take. Once someone from outside has asked directly, not forwarding it becomes the more awkward choice. And between the two options, most people choose to forward rather than hand out a colleague's direct email.

One Call, One Simple Question

To be clear, this isn't about cold-calling aggressively or repeatedly. It's one call, one simple, low-pressure question — designed to make it easy for the person on the other end to say yes.

If outreach in Japan feels like it's landing nowhere, it might not be the market saying no. It might be that the ask wasn't easy enough to act on.
